# Behavioral checks

Use these to validate changes to this skill, not as a checklist to print with every review. Evaluate decisions and reader effects rather than exact output wording. Synthetic fixtures below are tests, not samples of Aman's writing.

## Cases

1. **Clear prose in an unfamiliar genre.** A quiet personal paragraph or formal operational memo whose voice differs from the book should survive review unchanged when it is clear. No mandatory hook, anecdote, bolding, second example, or cadence adjustment.
2. **Local ambiguity.** “Maya sent Priya the revised forecast after she corrected the churn estimate.” With no other context, identify the unclear actor. Ask who corrected it or provide conditional alternatives; do not silently pick Maya or Priya.
3. **Necessary qualification.** “In our six interviews, two participants said the setup was confusing. That suggests a problem worth testing; it does not tell us how common the problem is.” Preserve the sample size, uncertainty, and distinction between observed and inferred. Do not turn this into “Users find setup confusing.”
4. **Duplicated function.** Two passages explain the same mechanism with the same example. Identify both, determine whether the later one adds a distinct consequence, and suggest a targeted cut or differentiation. Do not reorganize the entire piece.
5. **Missing author evidence.** A draft says a launch doubled revenue without support. Flag the measurement/attribution needed; never add a study, number, or causal claim to repair it.
6. **Useful variation.** A conversational aside, technical use of “silently,” an intentional long sentence, or a rhetorical question in an interview is not an automatic defect. A finding requires a problem in context.
7. **Track-change contamination.** Text contains “followedbeen using” from flattened alternatives. Request/use a coherent version; do not diagnose this as the author's natural writing error.
8. **Read-only review.** Deliver suggestions without applying them, posting comments, resolving discussions, or changing skill rules.

## Evidence of success

The review anchors its findings accurately, catches consequential clarity problems, abstains on effective prose, produces smaller and clearer alternatives, and preserves meaning, voice, and intent. Missing a low-impact copyedit is preferable to fabricating a substantive issue. Numeric editorial scores are not validated by these checks.

For calibration against real edits, hold out source passages and the human editor's feedback from the evaluating agent. Give the agent the unedited passage with enough context and the skill; compare its independent output afterward for the underlying reader problem, not wording similarity. An agreement is evidence on that case, not a measured general accuracy rate. Also include unfamiliar-genre and no-edit cases so recall does not reward overediting.
